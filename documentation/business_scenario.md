# Business Scenario

## Overview

An organisation has accumulated a large employee dataset containing information about its workforce.

The dataset includes demographic, employment, compensation, performance, workload, development, satisfaction and resignation information.

While the organisation has access to this data, HR leadership requires a structured analysis to understand the workforce more effectively and identify areas that may require management attention.

The HR team therefore needs to move beyond basic employee records and examine the broader patterns contained within the workforce data.

---

## Business Problem

The organisation currently lacks a consolidated analytical view of its workforce.

Management needs to understand:

- Who makes up the workforce?
- How is the workforce distributed across departments and roles?
- How does compensation vary across employee groups?
- How are employees performing?
- What does employee workload look like?
- How frequently are employees receiving promotions?
- How much training are employees receiving?
- How satisfied are employees?
- What proportion of employees are resigning?
- Are resignation patterns associated with particular workforce characteristics?

Without this analysis, HR leadership may have difficulty identifying workforce trends, potential retention risks and areas where employee development or workforce policies could be improved.

---

## Business Scenario

The HR leadership team has requested an analytical review of the employee population.

The analysis will use the available employee-level data to develop a clearer understanding of workforce characteristics and employee outcomes.

The project will examine relationships between workforce characteristics and outcomes such as:

- Performance
- Compensation
- Promotion
- Satisfaction
- Resignation

The objective is not to establish unsupported causal relationships, but to identify meaningful patterns and relationships that warrant management attention.

---

## Analytical Perspective

The project will approach the workforce from several perspectives:

### Workforce Composition

Understanding who the employees are and how they are distributed.

### Compensation

Understanding salary patterns across workforce segments.

### Performance

Examining employee performance and its relationship with other workforce characteristics.

### Productivity & Workload

Examining working hours, overtime, projects and workload indicators.

### Career Development

Examining training and promotion patterns.

### Employee Experience

Examining employee satisfaction and related workforce characteristics.

### Retention

Examining resignation patterns and identifying workforce segments with differing resignation rates.

---

## Expected Business Value

The analysis is expected to help HR leadership:

- Understand the current workforce structure.
- Identify important workforce patterns.
- Highlight potential retention concerns.
- Understand differences in compensation and career progression.
- Identify areas for further investigation.
- Support evidence-based HR decision-making.

The analysis and recommendations will be based on observed patterns in the data rather than assumptions about employee behaviour.
