# Insights & Business Recommendations

## Overview

This document records the business insights and recommendations developed during **Phase 5 — Insights & Business Recommendations** of the HR Workforce Analytics project.

The recommendations are based on the validated findings from:

- Phase 2 — Data Preparation & Excel Data Model
- Phase 3 — Workforce Analysis
- Phase 4 — HR Reporting & Dashboard

The purpose of this phase is to translate descriptive workforce findings into practical HR recommendations while remaining within the limitations of the available data.

The analytical reasoning framework is:

> **Finding → Evidence → Business Meaning → Recommendation**

The findings in this project describe observed patterns and associations within a synthetic workforce dataset.

They should not be interpreted as evidence of causal relationships unless supported by additional analysis or an appropriate research design.

---

# 1. Compensation and Performance

## Finding

Average monthly salary increases progressively across the performance categories.

| Performance Category | Average Monthly Salary |
|---|---:|
| Very Low | 5,422.23 |
| Low | 5,900.28 |
| Moderate | 6,409.52 |
| High | 6,897.63 |
| Very High | 7,397.67 |

This represents the clearest observed segment-level pattern in the workforce analysis.

## Business Insight

The organisation shows a clear observed association between higher performance categories and higher average monthly compensation.

This pattern provides an important compensation benchmark and suggests that performance and compensation should continue to be monitored together.

The analysis does not establish that performance directly causes salary differences.

## Recommendation

HR should periodically review compensation across performance categories to confirm that salary progression remains consistent with organisational reward practices and internal compensation principles.

Future analysis could also examine:

- Salary differences within the same department.
- Salary differences across job titles.
- Salary progression over time.
- Compensation variation within performance categories.

# 2. Career Development and Promotion

## Finding

The overall promotion rate is:

**66.70%**

Promotion rates remained broadly consistent across the tenure groups, departments and performance categories examined.

Average training hours were also relatively consistent across the major workforce segments.

## Business Insight

Promotion incidence is high across the workforce, but the descriptive analysis does not identify a specific workforce segment with a substantially different promotion outcome.

The consistency of promotion rates is therefore more useful as a monitoring result than as evidence of an immediate segment-specific problem.

## Recommendation

HR should continue monitoring promotion patterns but expand future analysis beyond promotion incidence alone.

Additional measures could include:

- Time to promotion.
- Number of years before first promotion.
- Promotion frequency by career stage.
- Role progression after promotion.
- Post-promotion performance outcomes.

These measures could provide a more detailed understanding of career development effectiveness.

---

# 3. Retention and Resignation

## Finding

The overall resignation rate is:

**10.01%**

A total of **10,010 employees** had resigned.

Resignation rates remained relatively close to the overall workforce rate across the segments examined, including:

- Department
- Gender
- Age group
- Tenure group
- Salary band
- Performance category
- Promotion status
- Satisfaction group
- Overtime group

No single segment showed a substantially different resignation rate within the descriptive analysis.

## Business Insight

The available analysis does not support focusing retention interventions on one specific department, demographic group or workforce segment.

The relatively uniform resignation rates suggest that the factors associated with resignation may be broader or may require additional information that is not available in the current dataset.

## Recommendation

HR should maintain organisation-wide retention monitoring rather than directing major interventions toward a single segment without stronger evidence.

Future analysis should consider additional explanatory information such as:

- Reasons for resignation.
- Exit interview results.
- Manager or leadership factors.
- Career progression opportunities.
- Compensation changes over time.
- Job role characteristics.
- Employee engagement measures.
- External labour market conditions.

This would provide a stronger basis for investigating potential drivers of employee resignation.

---

# 4. Employee Experience and Workload

## Finding

Resignation rates across satisfaction groups ranged from approximately:

**9.52% to 10.33%**

Resignation rates across overtime groups ranged from approximately:

**9.85% to 10.18%**

These differences remained relatively close to the overall resignation rate of **10.01%**.

## Business Insight

The descriptive analysis does not identify a single satisfaction or overtime category as a major retention-risk segment.

This means that the available evidence does not support assuming that low satisfaction or high overtime, by themselves, explain employee resignation within this dataset.

## Recommendation

HR should avoid designing major retention interventions around a single satisfaction or workload category without additional evidence.

Future analysis could investigate combinations of factors, such as:

- Satisfaction and overtime together.
- Satisfaction and promotion status.
- Workload and department.
- Overtime and remote work frequency.
- Performance and career progression.

This may provide more insight than examining each variable independently.

# 5. Workforce Monitoring and Management

## Finding

Several workforce outcomes showed limited variation across the major segments examined.

This includes:

- Promotion rates.
- Training hours.
- Satisfaction-related resignation rates.
- Overtime-related resignation rates.
- Resignation rates across most demographic and organisational segments.

## Business Insight

The absence of large differences is itself a meaningful analytical finding.

Management should not create targeted interventions simply because small numerical differences exist between workforce groups.

The available evidence suggests that the completed dashboard should be used as an ongoing monitoring tool to identify meaningful changes as workforce conditions evolve.

## Recommendation

The HR dashboard should be reviewed periodically to monitor changes in:

- Workforce composition.
- Compensation.
- Performance.
- Promotion.
- Resignation.

Targeted investigation should be prioritised when future data shows meaningful deviations from established workforce benchmarks.

---

# 6. Overall Business Recommendation

The strongest practical recommendation from the project is to adopt a **monitor, investigate and target** approach.

## Monitor

Continue monitoring the key workforce indicators through the completed Excel dashboard.

Key indicators include:

- Total Employees.
- Average Monthly Salary.
- Median Monthly Salary.
- Promotion Rate.
- Resignation Rate.

## Investigate

Where meaningful changes or deviations emerge, conduct more detailed analysis before identifying the cause or implementing a major intervention.

## Target

Apply targeted HR interventions only when the available evidence identifies a meaningful and actionable difference.

This approach helps ensure that workforce decisions are guided by evidence rather than small descriptive differences or unsupported assumptions.

---

# Key Phase 5 Conclusion

The Phase 5 analysis translated the validated workforce findings into practical HR insights and recommendations.

The clearest observed pattern remains the progressive association between performance category and average monthly salary.

Promotion incidence is high and broadly consistent across the workforce.

The overall resignation rate is **10.01%**, but no single segment examined showed a substantially different resignation rate within the descriptive analysis.

The project therefore does not recommend immediate large-scale interventions directed at a specific department, demographic group, satisfaction level or workload category.

Instead, the recommendations emphasise:

- Continued workforce monitoring.
- Periodic review of compensation and performance patterns.
- Deeper investigation of career progression.
- Additional analysis of potential resignation drivers.
- Evidence-based targeting of future HR interventions.

These recommendations are based on the available analytical evidence and do not present observed associations as causal relationships.
