# Project Objectives

## Primary Objective

The primary objective of the HR Workforce Analytics project is to analyse employee-level workforce data and translate the findings into meaningful HR insights that can support workforce planning, employee management and retention decisions.

---

## Supporting Objectives

The project has six supporting objectives.

### 1. Understand Workforce Composition

Analyse the demographic and organisational structure of the workforce.

This includes examining:

- Age
- Gender
- Department
- Job title
- Education
- Tenure

---

### 2. Analyse Compensation

Examine employee salary patterns and differences across relevant workforce segments.

The analysis will consider:

- Salary distribution
- Salary bands
- Department
- Job title
- Tenure
- Performance

---

### 3. Evaluate Employee Performance

Examine how employees are distributed across performance levels and investigate relationships between performance and other workforce characteristics.

---

### 4. Examine Employee Workload and Productivity

Analyse available workload and productivity indicators, including:

- Working hours
- Overtime
- Projects handled
- Sick days
- Team size

The purpose is to understand workforce workload patterns and identify areas that may warrant further investigation.

---

### 5. Analyse Employee Development and Experience

Examine:

- Training
- Promotions
- Employee satisfaction

The objective is to understand patterns in employee development and experience.

---

### 6. Investigate Employee Retention

Analyse resignation patterns across different workforce segments.

The analysis will examine resignation in relation to factors such as:

- Age
- Department
- Salary
- Tenure
- Performance
- Promotions
- Satisfaction
- Workload

The analysis will identify associations and patterns rather than claiming causation where the dataset cannot support it.

---

## Business Questions

The project will ultimately seek to answer questions such as:

1. What does the overall workforce look like?
2. Which departments and job roles have the largest employee populations?
3. How is the workforce distributed by age, gender and tenure?
4. How are salaries distributed across the organisation?
5. Which workforce segments have higher or lower salary levels?
6. How are employees distributed across performance categories?
7. What proportion of employees have received promotions?
8. How does training vary across the workforce?
9. What does employee satisfaction look like?
10. How common is employee resignation?
11. Which workforce segments have higher resignation rates?
12. What relationships exist between resignation and employee characteristics?
13. Are there workforce areas that warrant management attention?
14. What practical actions could HR leadership consider based on the findings?

---

## Scope

### Included

The analysis will focus on:

- Employee demographics
- Workforce structure
- Compensation
- Performance
- Workload
- Productivity indicators
- Training
- Promotions
- Satisfaction
- Resignation

### Excluded

The project will not attempt to:

- Establish definitive causal relationships.
- Predict future resignation using machine learning.
- Evaluate individual employees for disciplinary purposes.
- Make unsupported claims about employee behaviour.
- Replace HR judgement with automated decisions.

---

## Success Criteria

The project will be considered successful when it produces:

1. A validated and analysis-ready employee dataset.
2. A structured workforce analysis.
3. Clear and interpretable HR metrics.
4. An executive-friendly reporting layer.
5. Evidence-based business insights.
6. Practical HR recommendations.
7. A documented and reproducible portfolio project.
